Prices above and below where we are now, closest first. The bar shows how far away each one is. ATR is just "a normal bar's range" — so 1.0 ATR away is about one candle's worth of move, and 5 ATR is a long way. ◆ means two different things point at the same price, which is worth more than either on its own. They are not ranked by how strong they are, only by how close: strength would need one number blending touches, freshness and volume, and those do not add up.

The point of control is where the most changed hands and tends to act as a magnet. The value area holds 70% of the volume and its edges are where price has been rejected back inside. LVNs are prices the market rejected — price moves fast through them, which makes them better targets than supports.

Market profile

One letter per half hour, printed at every price it traded through. Many letters at a price means the market accepted it; a single letter means it was rejected. The initial balance is the first hour — the opening auction everything after it is measured against. A poor high is an extreme with two or more letters on it: the auction stopped without a tail, which usually means price comes back for it.

Option chain
Where the size is, by strike

Puts left, calls right, strikes running down the page the way a chain is read. The pale bar is open interest — contracts that exist and must eventually be closed or settled. The solid bar inside it is what traded today. Big open interest with no volume is an old position sitting there; big volume with little open interest is something being opened right now, and those are different news.

Open interest by strike ■ calls ■ puts

How many contracts are open at each strike. The tallest bars are where the most money is parked, and price tends to be drawn towards them: the biggest call strike above spot acts as resistance, the biggest put strike below it as support.

IV smile ■ calls ■ puts

What the market charges for volatility at each strike. The curve normally lifts at both ends — the far strikes cost more per unit of risk. Whichever side lifts more is the side people are paying up to protect against.

Term structure ATM IV by expiry

The price of volatility at each expiry, nearest on the left. It normally slopes up — more can happen in a month than in a day. When a near expiry sits above the ones after it, the market is pricing an event inside that window, and selling volatility into it is how a quiet-looking trade goes wrong.

ATM IV last 24h

What a day of movement has been costing, over the last 24 hours. Rising means options are getting dearer — buyers are paying up, usually into uncertainty. Falling means premium is draining out and the market is calming. Compare it with the IV / RV tile above: that says whether the price is fair against how much the market has actually been moving.

Put / call OI last 24h

Open interest in puts divided by calls. Above 1 means more puts are open than calls — hedging or bearish positioning; below 1 the other way. The move matters more than the level: the ratio can sit wherever the book happens to sit for hours, so it is a rise or a fall over a few hours that says writers have switched sides.
